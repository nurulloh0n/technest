TechNest is the engineering team behind this site — a group of students who take an idea from a napkin sketch to a working 3D-modeled prototype. Every project starts the same way: pick a real problem, name it, build it, and be honest about what it does and doesn't do yet.

We work in monthly sprints. Each sprint ends with a "prototype card" — the same short, honest format you'll see below: what it is, how it works, and what it costs the world to run.

"A good prototype name is short, easy to say, and hints at what the machine does."

Vision

Machines should give more than they take.

We believe sustainability is a design constraint, not an afterthought — so every prototype has to answer for its own materials, power draw, and repairs before it earns a name.

01

Build honestly

Every card lists what a prototype saves and what it costs. No pluses without minuses.

02

Design for reuse

Water, paper, and power are treated as loops to close, not resources to spend once and discard.

03

Ship something real

A 3D file and a spec sheet aren't enough — a prototype has to actually collect, filter, sort, or store.

Prototype Outlook

Every TechNest build shares one architecture.

Underneath eight different jobs — catching water, pressing pulp, banking volts — the frame stays the same. Here's what's actually inside the shell.

  • Shell & frame

    Printed or laser-cut housing that sets the prototype's footprint and protects the internals.

  • Intake / sensor layer

    Where the machine meets the outside world — mesh, hopper, panel, or probe.

  • Process core

    The working part: a filter stack, a press, a battery cell, or a sorting arm.

  • Storage / output

    Tank, bin, cell, or hatch — where the result of the process is held or released.

  • Base mount

    Keeps the whole assembly stable and sets how it's installed on-site.
